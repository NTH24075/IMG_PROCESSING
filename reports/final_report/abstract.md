Counting people in fixed-camera surveillance images with background subtraction needs no training data and runs in milliseconds, but its counts degrade when illumination differs between the frame and the reference background, when cast shadows join the foreground, and when nearby pedestrians merge into one blob. We describe a training-free pipeline that targets each failure with one component: histogram matching of the frame to the background, a shadow test that combines the frame-to-background intensity ratio with a gradient difference, and a perspective-dependent area model that converts blob area into a person count, calibrated on development frames only. On 76 doubly reviewed development frames from two public scenes, the pipeline reduced mean absolute error (MAE) from 1.00 to 0.59 people per frame (95% block-bootstrap CI 0.26–0.97) relative to a one-blob-one-person baseline. It also outperformed a pre-trained HOG+SVM person detector (MAE 1.50; paired difference 0.91, CI 0.33–1.45) at 7.6 ms per frame instead of 216 ms. Ablation attributed this gain to the area model; on clean frames, the contributions of histogram matching and shadow suppression were within sampling error. Histogram matching instead mattered under synthetic photometric change, holding worst-case MAE at 0.61 for reduced contrast and 0.59 for gain change, against 13.5 and 4.2 for the baseline, whereas salt-and-pepper noise remained a failure mode (MAE 1.63).
