Learning-based people counters, from density regression to class-agnostic counting, depend on annotated training images. Background subtraction for fixed cameras needs no training, yet its counts fail when lighting changes, shadows join the foreground or neighbouring pedestrians merge into one blob. We tested whether three training-free corrections, each aimed at one of these failures, could recover counting accuracy while keeping millisecond-scale processing. The pipeline matches each frame's histogram to the reference background and rejects shadow pixels using an intensity ratio and a gradient difference. A perspective-dependent area model, fitted on development frames only, then converts each blob's area into a person count. Against a one-blob-one-person baseline, mean absolute error (MAE) on 76 doubly reviewed frames from two public scenes fell from 1.00 to 0.59 people per frame. A pre-trained HOG–SVM detector reached 1.50 on the same frames (paired difference 0.91; 95% block-bootstrap CI 0.33–1.45) and ran 28 times slower. Ablation attributed the clean-frame gain to the area model, because the effects of histogram matching and shadow rejection fell within sampling error. Under synthetic contrast reduction and gain change, histogram matching instead held MAE at 0.61 and 0.59, against 13.5 and 4.2 for the baseline. Salt-and-pepper noise remained a failure mode, raising the pipeline's MAE to 1.63.
